The invoice tracker I wish I had started with (and the 30-day rule)

Here is the uncomfortable arithmetic of an unpaid invoice. The work is done. The value was delivered. The client has the benefit of it. And every week the invoice sits unpaid, the chance of getting paid drops — not because anyone is being dishonest, but because memory fades, budgets close, and someone else's problem takes priority over yours.

Why the tracker is not about bookkeeping

Most people build an invoice tracker to know how much money they are owed. That is useful, but it is not the point. The point is the aging: how old the oldest invoice is, and which client it belongs to.

Because there are only two kinds of unpaid invoice:

  • Old and quiet. More than thirty days, no reply. Something is wrong and nobody is telling you.
  • New. Less than thirty days. Completely normal. Leave it alone.

The 30-day rule

If an invoice is more than thirty days old, you chase it today. Not tomorrow, not at the end of the week, not when you feel like it. Today, and only because it is thirty days old.

Not because thirty days is a magic number, but because after thirty days the invoice is no longer a fresh request — it is a reminder that something went wrong, and it is much easier to raise it while the answer is still cheap to give.

What the tracker needs

Five columns get you ninety percent of the value:

  1. Number — so you can refer to it in one word.
  2. Client — a relation, so one click shows everything else they owe.
  3. Amount and currency — including what is still outstanding.
  4. Date issued — the number the aging is calculated from.
  5. Days overdue — a formula, not a mental calculation.

Then one aging table that sorts by days overdue, worst first. That table is the whole point. It turns a list into a to-do list.

The chase email

Keep one. Write it once, save it, send it on a schedule instead of when you start worrying:

Hi [Name] — quick one. Invoice [NUMBER] for [AMOUNT] is still open, due [DATE]. Here is the link: [link]. Can you let me know when it will be paid?

No apology. No explanation of how busy you are. Just the invoice, the date, and one question. It is much easier to answer than a paragraph, and it keeps the relationship professional.
