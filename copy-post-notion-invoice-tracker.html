A Notion invoice tracker that tells you which client to chase

Notion is very good at being a filing cabinet. It is less good at being a system — and that is the whole difference between a workspace you look at and one that runs your business.

The six databases

Clients, Projects, Invoices, Tasks, Time Log, Testimonials. Every one of them is related to the others, so a single click on a client shows you their projects, their invoices, their tasks and their words about you.

The view that matters most

Not the dashboard. The invoice aging view.

Sorted by days overdue, worst first, with the client name pulled in from the relation rather than typed again. It is the one screen that answers the only question that matters on a Monday morning: who do I need to email today?

The formula is simple — today minus the due date — but the effect is not. A number that sits in a list gets ignored. A number sorted to the top gets acted on.

The formulas that are already written

  • Days overdue
  • Total billed, total paid, total outstanding per client
  • Effective hourly rate, per client and per project
  • Budget used, against a project budget property
  • Days since last contact

Each one is explained in the setup guide, so you are copying working expressions instead of debugging them at eleven at night.

Setup takes about twenty minutes

Create the six databases, paste in the properties, add the formulas, import the six CSV files that come with it, and relate the databases to each other. The guide walks through it in the order that avoids confusion.

It works on the free Notion plan. No paid tier, no add-ons, no external automations — the formulas do the work that most people pay a tool to do.
