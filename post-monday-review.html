The 20-minute Monday review that keeps a solo business from drifting

SoloForge · weekly review freelance, solo business checklist, freelance weekly review

Here is the thing nobody tells you about running a business alone: the work does not fail because you did it badly. It fails because you forgot to do it. The proposal you postponed. The invoice you did not chase. The client who went quiet because nobody followed up. Each of those is a small omission, and small omissions compound.

The fix is not discipline. It is a twenty-minute appointment with yourself, every Monday, where you answer six questions in writing.

The six questions

1. For every active client — what is happening next? A name with no next action is not a client, it is a hope. Write the next action and the date for each one. If you cannot write the date, the project has no shape yet.

2. What is overdue, and what is due this week? Two separate lists. Overdue is money you should already have; due this week is work that leaves if nobody moves it. They need different energy, so do not mix them.

3. What ships this week, and what is actually stuck? For each thing you promised, name the one thing that would unblock it. If you cannot name it, it is not stuck — it is vague. Rewrite the task until the blocker is obvious.

4. Am I over capacity? Count the hours already committed. If the total is above what you can deliver without degrading everything else, the answer is a scope conversation, not a longer week. The sentence that works: "This week I can fit one more thing. Which one do you want to drop?"

5. What are the three things that must be true for this week to be good? Not nine things. Three. If everything is a priority, nothing is.

6. What one thing am I going to stop doing? This is the whole trick. Every week, one thing stops. That is how the list stays short instead of becoming a graveyard of good intentions. It might be a meeting you do not need, a client that does not pay, or a project that quietly stopped being worth it.

Why writing it down matters

Because you cannot answer a question from memory that you have never written down. The first time you run this review you will discover that you do not know what is overdue, or how much money is in the bank, or which client has been waiting three weeks for a reply. That is not a discipline problem. It is a systems problem, and it has a one-page fix.

The review is twenty minutes. The alternative is finding out in six months.
