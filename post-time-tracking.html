Track your time for one month. It will change what you charge.

SoloForge · freelance time tracker, billable hours tracker, track time as a freelancer

Run this experiment for one month. Add one column to whatever you already track — a spreadsheet, a Notion database, a paper notebook — and mark every hour as either billable or not. That is the whole experiment.

What you are looking for

At the end of the month, divide your billable hours by your total hours. For most solo professionals the answer is somewhere between two-thirds and four-fifths.

The missing fifth is not a failure. It is simply invisible until you measure it, and because it is invisible, you have been pricing it away.

Where the time actually goes

From my own logs, the non-billable categories, in rough order of size:

  • Scoping and discovery. The calls before you have quoted. Real work, usually unpaid.
  • Revisions. Often unnamed in the quote and unlimited in practice.
  • Sales. The conversation that won the job, plus every conversation that did not.
  • Admin. Invoicing, chasing, contracts, bookkeeping.
  • Admin you chose. The weekly review, the learning, the tooling. This is the good kind — do not cut it.

Two changes that follow

Your rate goes up. If a fifth of the work is not billable, then a quote based on total hours is really a quote for four-fifths of the work. Put the number in the quote.

You start declining. Here is the part that took me two years to notice: the projects with the worst ratio of billable to admin are almost never the projects that look the most impressive on paper. They are the ones where the client email is vague, the decision-maker is unclear, and the brief is going to move. Those are not creative projects. They are project management with a creative label, and they are the first thing to stop accepting once you can see the ratio.

Keep it light

Do not build a complicated system. One sheet, one date column, one client column, one project column, one billable column. If tracking takes more than two minutes a day, you will stop, and then you have learned nothing.
